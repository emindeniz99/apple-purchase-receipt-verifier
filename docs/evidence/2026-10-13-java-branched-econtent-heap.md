# Java: the heap cost of a branched constructed-string eContent

Date: 2026-10-13. Measures what a receipt costs the Java implementation
when its eContent is a branched tree of constructed (BER) OCTET STRINGs,
for owner decision Q131 (record it, no refusal) and the per-call heap
figure in java/README.md and THREAT-MODEL.md §3.7. Sources and commands:
[`2026-10-13-java-branched-econtent-heap/`](2026-10-13-java-branched-econtent-heap/).

## Question

`ReceiptCore.parseCms` hands the whole receipt to
`ASN1Primitive.fromByteArray` before any signer is matched. BouncyCastle's
`BEROctetString` keeps a flattened copy of its content beside its
children, so each internal node of a branched tree of constructed OCTET
STRINGs holds a copy of its whole subtree. A security review measured
about 77 MiB retained after parsing such a tree and set it against the
66 MiB per call that java/README.md sizes the heap by. How much heap does
the shape need through the public API, at the receipt cap, by the method
behind the 66 MiB figure?

## Setup

- OpenJDK 21.0.10 (Ubuntu build), default G1, 4 vCPU, 16 GB RAM, one
  thread. bcprov, bcutil and bcpkix 1.86, jackson-core 2.22.3.
- The library at `main` `1cb08f0e`.
- The method of
  [`2026-10-05-java-presignature-parse-cost.md`](2026-10-05-java-presignature-parse-cost.md):
  the smallest `-Xmx` at which one `verifyReceipt` call in a fresh JVM
  completes, bisected to 1 MiB three times, less the baseline's. The
  baseline and tiny-attributes inputs are that note's, regenerated by its
  `PresignatureCost make` on this tree (`results/make.txt`).
- Every tree is definite-length. Its root has 2, 3 or 5 children, every
  other constructed node two; leaves are primitive OCTET STRINGs of zero
  bytes. It sits in a `TestPki` receipt's envelope with that receipt's
  certificates and SignerInfo, so nothing signs it; under Apple's roots
  each call ends at `UNTRUSTED_CHAIN`.

## Results

From `results/run.txt`. The three bisections agreed on every input.

| Input | DER bytes | Base64 chars | Smallest `-Xmx`, MiB | Over the baseline, MiB |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `baseline` | 3,123 | 4,164 | 13 | 0 |
| `tiny-attributes` | 2,359,255 | 3,145,676 | 79 | 66 |
| tree: root 5, 17 levels, leaf 3 | 2,327,225 | 3,102,968 | 69 | 56 |
| tree: root 2, 18 levels, leaf 4 | 2,124,512 | 2,832,684 | 67 | 54 |
| tree: root 3, 18 levels, leaf 1 | 2,005,707 | 2,674,276 | 65 | 52 |
| tree: root 5, 16 levels, leaf 10 | 2,327,225 | 3,102,968 | 63 | 50 |
| tree: root 2, 19 levels, leaf 0 | 2,124,512 | 2,832,684 | 59 | 46 |
| tree: root 2, 17 levels, leaf 8 | 1,600,192 | 2,133,592 | 51 | 38 |

The worst tree tried needs 56 MiB more than a genuine receipt, 19 times
its base64. The tiny-attributes receipt's 66 MiB stays the largest
figure measured, so the README's per-call figure stands.

The review's two shapes do not fit the cap. As receipts they are
4,263,552 base64 characters (root 2, 19 levels, leaf 2) and 3,564,504
(root 2, 18 levels, leaf 6), refused as `TOO_LARGE` before anything is
parsed. Its 77 MiB was also a different measure, the heap retained after
`fromByteArray` alone.

## Verdict

The branched tree is a second mechanism behind the same residual risk as
the tiny attributes: work before trust that only the input cap bounds.
It costs less than the attribute flood at the cap, and the same
mitigation, a body cap and a concurrency limit at the edge, bounds it.
Apple sends DER, and the core refuses constructed strings past six
levels; Java's bound is BouncyCastle's nesting depth, 64 by default.
No code changes.

## Where this stops holding

- One machine, one JDK, one collector. The bisected heap includes the
  JVM's floor (13 MiB here) and the caller's copy of the base64.
- bcprov 1.86. A BouncyCastle that stops keeping the flattened copy, or
  starts keeping more, changes every tree figure.
- Six shapes. The search varied the root's children, the depth and the
  leaf size under the cap; it is not a proof that no tree costs more.
